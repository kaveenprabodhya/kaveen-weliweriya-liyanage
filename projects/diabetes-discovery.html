Problem

A staged public-health modelling study over CDC survey data that combines risk classification, class-imbalance experiments, explainability, population clustering and association analysis.

Contribution

The project answers two different analytical questions with two different pipelines: who appears at higher diabetes risk, and what population structures exist without using the outcome label. Separate preprocessing prevents the clustering representation from becoming a by-product of the classifier.

How the system is structured

Engineering depth

  • Separate paradigms, separate preprocessing: classification and clustering do not reuse transformations simply for convenience; each branch prepares data for its own objective.
  • Cross-validation before test evaluation: model comparison and Optuna search happen before the selected configuration is inspected on the held-out test data.
  • Imbalance is treated explicitly: the repository keeps a dedicated oversampling experiment and reports macro/class metrics rather than depending on aggregate accuracy.
  • Probability quality is considered: calibration variants, ROC/PR measures and threshold analyses appear alongside hard classifications.
  • Triangulated analysis: predictive modelling, explainability, clustering and association rules reduce the temptation to treat one model's feature importance as the whole story.
